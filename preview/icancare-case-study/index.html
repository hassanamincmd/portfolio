Case study · Healthcare · Novartis

ICan Care

A hybrid mobile app for treatment care, trusted resources, and clinical research.

Articles, reminders, and healthcare provider listings for patients and caregivers, with secure feedback collection for analytics and clinical studies.

Client
Novartis · Cancer support
Role
UI/UX Designer
Platform
Hybrid mobile · iOS & Android
Research
Interviews · Surveys · Contextual inquiry

01 · Challenges & objectives

The goal was clear: build a hybrid app with articles, reminders, and provider listings while gathering user feedback for analytics and clinical research. Translating high-level requirements into actionable specs meant every design decision had to serve both usability and security, especially when handling sensitive medical data.

Dual mandate

Support patients on hard days while collecting anonymized signal for researchers. Neither audience could be an afterthought.

Usability vs. security

A robust feature set had to coexist with a seamless experience and strict data protection for thousands of simultaneous users.

Specification depth

High-level stakeholder requirements needed to become detailed, build-ready flows without losing the human context behind each feature.

Project objectives

Effortless care management

Empower users to manage appointments and medications without cognitive overload.

Curated awareness

Provide accessible, trustworthy articles that enhance lifestyle and treatment understanding.

Research-ready data

Enable efficient collection for clinical studies and analytics without compromising the daily experience.

Scale & security

Ensure the platform could scale securely with robust encryption and zero tolerance for data breaches.

02 · Discovery & strategy

Through stakeholder interviews, surveys, and contextual inquiry, two primary audiences emerged. Users demanded clear reminders, simple navigation, and data privacy as a non-negotiable requirement.

Hannah

Cancer survivor

Goals

Stay informed about treatments and manage appointments without missing medication windows.

Pain points

Often forgot medication times and struggled to find trustworthy information online.

Dr. Ravi

The researcher

Goals

Analyze patient data to identify trends for clinical trials and improve care pathways.

Pain points

Needed anonymized, compliant data while meeting strict privacy regulations.

Research themes

Theme 01

Personalization

Tailor content and reminders to each user’s medical journey.

Theme 02

Transparency

Provide clear, trustworthy information about clinical trials and healthcare providers.

Theme 03

Security

Ensure robust data encryption and privacy controls at every touchpoint.

Theme 04

Usability

Simplify sign-up, reminder setup, and article browsing into obvious workflows.

Navigation model

The home screen offered quick access to reminders, articles, and providers. Clinical trials and settings lived in separate sections so the daily care loop stayed focused. A later 2026 refresh refined this into five tabs with language-first onboarding for Malaysia (EN, BM, 中文, தமிழ்).

Home

Today

Greeting, search, next dose, doctor visit, and later today. The hub for the next action.

Learn

Guidance

Clinician-reviewed topics and featured articles, filterable by therapeutic area.

Reminders

Adherence

Progress for today, due-now decisions, and calendar-synced appointment schedules.

Nearby

Providers

Healthcare provider listings by distance, specialty, and rating.

Profile

Trust

History, saved articles, care-team contact, privacy, clinical trials, and settings.

Critical user flows

Sign-up & login

Register via email or social accounts with privacy expectations framed as care, not legal theatre.

Setting reminders

Appointments and medication schedules synced seamlessly with phone calendars.

Accessing content

Filter articles by therapeutic area with featured stories and topic grids for scanning.

03 · User interface

From mid-fidelity sketches to interactive high-fidelity prototypes, each iteration addressed user pain points. Core flows across onboarding, daily care, education, and trust.

Feature 01

Language onboarding

English, Bahasa Melayu, 中文, and தமிழ் as equal choices. Continue opens Home, not a wall of forms.

Feature 02

Home · next dose first

Quick access to reminders, articles, and providers. Mark as taken is the primary action. Surveys never compete with urgency.

Feature 03

Reminders · progress you can feel

Today’s progress as a simple strip. Due-now cards offer Taken and Later, synced with phone calendars.

Feature 04

Add medicine stepper

Name. Dose. Frequency. Confirm. A linear three-step flow so people leave with a saved medicine, not a half-filled form.

Feature 05

Learn hub

Featured article first, topic tiles second. Filter by therapeutic area for carers and common questions.

Feature 06

Article · read or listen

Hero image, save, audio progress strip, and checklist sections. Empathy without pretending the app is a clinician.

Feature 07

Nearby providers

Search plus filter chips. Each row shows rating, distance, department, and a hospital photo.

Feature 08

Profile & privacy

Medicine history, saved articles, care-team contact, privacy & consent, and settings in one quiet place.

04 · Testing & outcomes

Prototypes were tested on both Android and iOS across three rounds of usability testing, plus a heuristic evaluation for error prevention, aesthetics, and efficiency.

Navigation hierarchy

Early builds buried key features. Information architecture was refined until reminders, articles, and providers were one tap away.

Reminder setup

Mobile responsiveness and the medicine setup flow were optimized until the daily loop felt obvious.

92% task success

Final testing confirmed the design’s effectiveness. Remaining gaps were small and documented for engineering.

05 · Reflections

This project reinforced that thoughtful design can transform lives, especially when balancing technical constraints with human needs on hard days.

Reflection 01

Early user involvement pays off

Stakeholder interviews and iterative testing caught navigation and setup issues before they reached production.

Reflection 02

Privacy is a product requirement

For medical data, consent and encryption change what you ask, when you ask, and how you store the answer.

Reflection 03

Two audiences, two speeds

Hannah lives on Home and Reminders. Dr. Ravi lives on optional research paths. Mixing those speeds on one screen helps neither.

Reflection 04

Calm UI is a clinical choice

Balancing a robust feature set with a seamless experience was challenging but rewarding. Whitespace and honest copy are not decoration.
