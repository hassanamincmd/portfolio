Case study · Super App

Meridian

A multi-user, cross-platform app for scheduling, events, and public profiles.

I led design for Meridian, covering calendar organization, smart booking, and a modular public profile builder for independent professionals across web, tablet, and mobile.

Duration
12 months
Team
3 Product Designers
Platforms
Web · Tablet · Mobile
Role
Design Lead

01 · The problem

Independent professionals in creative, lifestyle, and healthcare industries were running a business and doing the work simultaneously, across fragmented tools with no unified workflow.

“I don’t need another app. I need the apps I already use to talk to each other, and I need my clients to find me without explaining what I do every single time.”
Primary research insight, stakeholder sessions

Scheduling fragmentation

Professionals manually synced multiple calendars, chased confirmations, and blocked availability by hand, work that had nothing to do with their actual craft.

Broken booking experience

Clients had no unified path from discovery to booking: no single place to understand services, view live availability, and confirm.

Invisible professional identity

A static link-in-bio page couldn’t carry the full weight of a professional’s identity, services, or real-time availability.

02 · The goal & process

A single intelligent platform consolidating scheduling, booking, client management, and personal branding, with a design system first, then high-velocity validated output.

Phase 1 · Discovery

Stakeholder interviews, three professional archetypes (Creative, Healthcare, Lifestyle), and journey maps for both professional and client paths.

Phase 2 · Design system

Token-based library before feature work: cards, modals, forms, empty states, and cross-platform variants that multiplied team velocity.

Phase 3 · Execution

Weekly critiques, stakeholder validation gates before handoff, and clear feature ownership so three designers could work in parallel.

03 · User interface

Six major feature areas, each designed for a distinct mental model, validated with stakeholders, and built from the shared component library.

Feature 01

Home dashboard

Three schedule views with a persistent toggle: Calendar, Board/Gantt, and Table, so professionals could inhabit their work the way they actually think.

Feature 02

Smart booking profile

Two-panel layout: identity and social proof on the left; live calendar picker on the right.

Feature 03

Profile section builder

Modular drag-and-drop sections: hero, services, gallery, testimonials, available times.

Feature 04

Analytics

Summary, trend, and granular breakdown views, with every metric mapped to a stakeholder question.

Feature 05

Settings & billing

Organized settings architecture and an upgrade modal designed to reduce anxiety at checkout.

Feature 06

Authentication

Focused login with email/password, recovery path, and SSO via Google, Apple, and Outlook, treated as a brand moment, not a gate.

04 · Outcomes

Across 12 months and six major feature areas, the work delivered at both the product and team level. The product was discontinued by executive decision before reaching live production. The work here reflects the full design lifecycle during the active phase.

Outcome 01

Stakeholder trust

+80% satisfaction measured from the Design Lead transition, a direct signal of improved design quality, process rigor, and cross-functional alignment.

Outcome 02

Output velocity

20 screens per sprint vs. 6 prior, a 3× increase driven by the mature design system and clearer handoff protocol.

Outcome 03

Cross-platform delivery

Web, tablet, and mobile from a single token-based system with platform-specific component variants.

Outcome 04

Unified language

Where fragmented patterns existed, one design system emerged, enabling three designers to work in parallel without divergence.

05 · Reflections

Reflection 01

The design system paid back every week

The upfront cost felt like a slowdown in months one and two. By month four, it was the single biggest multiplier on team velocity and output quality.

Reflection 02

Stakeholder alignment as accelerator

Structured, frequent reviews tied to specific decision questions turned stakeholder sessions into a velocity tool, not a bottleneck.

Reflection 03

Ownership raised the floor

Clear feature ownership created accountability and deep expertise, dramatically improving handoff quality by project end.

Reflection 04

Design ops as much as design

The invisible output, shared language, validated process, and confidence to push back, is the work I’m most proud of on this project.
