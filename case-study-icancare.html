.01 — Where It Started

A hybrid app with
two jobs at once.

The brief was to create a hybrid mobile app that offered articles, reminders, and healthcare-provider listings — while also gathering user feedback for analytics and clinical research. Achieving that meant translating high-level requirements into specs that were usable for patients and secure enough for medical data.

Hero — pink ribbon, ICan Care wordmark, Novartis mobile application assets/icancare-hero.png
01 — dual mandate

Support people. Feed research.

Patients needed appointments, medications, and trustworthy articles. Researchers needed anonymized data. The product had to serve both without making either feel like an afterthought.

02 — sensitive data

Usability could not outrun security

Every flow — sign-up, reminders, surveys — had to handle medical information with encryption, clear consent, and privacy as a non-negotiable, not a settings toggle.

03 — scale

Simple enough for daily use

The app needed to stay calm under a full feature set and remain usable for thousands of simultaneous users — not a dense clinical tool wearing a consumer skin.

.02 — Objectives

Four outcomes,
one product.

With the constraints named, the work was aimed at four objectives that later became the information architecture.

01

Effortless schedules

Help people manage appointments and medications without hunting through calendars or paper lists.

02

Trustworthy content

Offer curated, accessible articles that raise awareness and support lifestyle — filterable by therapeutic area.

03

Research-ready data

Collect anonymized feedback and polls in a way that is useful for clinical studies without burdening the patient.

04

Security at scale

Design for encryption, privacy compliance, and thousands of concurrent sessions from the first screens, not a later audit.

.03 — Discovery

Two audiences.
One privacy bar.

Stakeholder interviews, surveys, and contextual inquiry surfaced two primary groups. Both demanded clear reminders, simple navigation, and data privacy they could believe.

patients & caregivers

Manage treatment, stay informed

They needed help with treatment schedules and a way to find trustworthy information without wading through the open web.

clinicians & researchers

Anonymized signal, not extra work

They needed anonymized data for clinical studies and analysis — without compromising the patient’s experience or legal privacy requirements.

.05 — Design Strategy

Four themes from
the research wall.

Affinity mapping turned findings into a strategy the rest of the screens had to obey.

personalization

Tailor the journey

Content and reminders follow each user’s medical path — not a generic broadcast.

transparency

Say what is true

Clear, trustworthy information about clinical trials and healthcare providers. No buried disclaimers.

security

Encrypt by default

Robust data encryption and privacy language in the product, not only in a policy PDF.

usability

Shorten the path

Simplify sign-up, reminders, and browsing articles until the daily loop feels obvious.

.09 — Testing & Outcomes

Iterate until the
task succeeds.

Three rounds of usability testing, plus a heuristic pass for error prevention, aesthetics, and efficiency. Navigation hierarchy and reminder setup were the biggest repairs.

findings

Hierarchy and setup

Navigation was hard to scan. Reminder setup felt long. Both were simplified before launch.

platforms

Android and iOS

Responsive behavior was checked on both so the hybrid shell did not hide platform-specific friction.

validation

92% task success

Final testing reached a 92% task success rate. Heuristics confirmed the remaining gaps were small.

.10 — Reflections

What I’d carry forward.

Involve users before the screens harden.

Early interviews and three test rounds saved the information architecture. Waiting until high fidelity would have made the same findings expensive.

Privacy is a product requirement.

For medical data, encryption and anonymization are not a compliance appendix. They change what you ask, when you ask, and how you store the answer.

Two audiences need two speeds.

Patients live on home and reminders. Researchers live on polls and exports. Mixing those speeds on one screen helps neither.

Calm UI is a clinical choice.

Whitespace, short copy, and honest disclaimers are not decoration here. People open this app on hard days. The interface should not add weight.

This project reinforced that thoughtful design can change daily life — if you keep testing until the task, not the mockup, is the measure.
