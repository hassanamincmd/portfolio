.01 Where It Started

Care on hard days.
Research without the weight.

The original brief asked for a hybrid app that helped people manage treatment while optionally feeding clinical research. The 2026 refresh kept that dual mandate, but rebuilt the daily experience around calm, clarity, and language.

Hero collage, Onboarding + Home side by side, medical blue system assets/icancare-hero.png

Drop a full-bleed hero here when ready. Used on this page and the homepage project card.

01 dual mandate

Support people. Feed research.

Patients needed doses, appointments, and guidance they could trust. Researchers needed anonymized signal. Neither job could crowd out the other on a hard day.

02 language & access

Care starts in your language

In Malaysia, English-only onboarding is a barrier. The product had to open in EN, Bahasa Melayu, 中文, or தமிழ் before asking for an account.

03 cognitive load

One decision at a time

Medication apps fail when every screen asks for everything. The interface needed urgency without panic, and depth without dumping it on Home.

.02 Product Strategy

Five tabs.
One mental model.

Information architecture follows how people think about a care day: what’s due now, what to learn, where to go, and who I am. Research stays optional and off the critical path.

home

Today

Greeting, search, next dose, doctor, later today. The hub for the next action.

learn

Guidance

Clinician-reviewed topics and featured articles, without pretending to replace a care team.

reminders

Adherence

Progress for today, due-now decisions, and a short path to add a medicine.

nearby

Centres

Find oncology support by distance, specialty, and rating, not a raw map dump.

profile

Trust

History, saved articles, care-team contact, privacy, and settings in one quiet place.

01

Language before account

Onboarding starts with preference, not paperwork. Flags and labels make the choice scannable under stress.

02

Medical blue, not clinical grey

A blue hero system signals care and trust while soft surfaces and shadows keep the body of the app calm.

03

Empathy in the copy

“Find care that fits your day” and “Let’s find your care” set tone before any feature list does.

04

Accessible by default

48px targets, labeled controls, decorative imagery marked hidden, and status patterns that survive light and dark heroes.

.04 Adherence

Progress you can feel.
Decisions you can finish.

Reminders turn a chaotic medicine list into a day view: how far you’ve gotten, what is due now, and a short stepper when something new needs adding.

01 reminders

Taken or later. Nothing else.

Today’s progress shows as a simple strip. Due-now cards offer Taken and Later. Later today lists meds and telehealth without burying the next action.

.08 Testing & Outcomes

Iterate until the
task succeeds.

Three rounds of usability testing plus a heuristic pass. Navigation hierarchy and reminder setup were the biggest repairs before launch.

findings

Hierarchy and setup

Early builds buried the next dose and stretched medicine setup. Both were shortened until the daily loop felt obvious.

platforms

Android and iOS

The hybrid shell was checked on both platforms so platform friction did not hide behind a single mockup.

validation

92% task success

Final testing reached a 92% task success rate. Remaining gaps were small and documented for engineering.

.09 Reflections

What I’d carry forward.

Language is an accessibility feature.

Starting in the right language is not localization polish. It is whether someone can use the product on day one.

Privacy is a product requirement.

For medical data, consent and encryption change what you ask, when you ask, and how you store the answer.

Two audiences need two speeds.

Patients live on Home and Reminders. Research lives on optional paths. Mixing those speeds on one screen helps neither.

Calm UI is a clinical choice.

Whitespace, short copy, and honest disclaimers are not decoration. People open this app on hard days.

The 2026 refresh proved the same brief can feel entirely different when the daily loop, not the feature list, becomes the measure.
