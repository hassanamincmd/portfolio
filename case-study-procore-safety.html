Safety Hub went to open beta on April 21, 2026, as part of Procore’s 2026 safety investment. Qualifications Management shipped into closed beta with a web hub and mobile Safety Cards. On the work I owned: a 12-person study rebuilt the cards people could not read, and a 3-tap pattern replaced jumping between Incidents, Observations, Inspections, and Hazards.

02 · The problem in one picture

Users assembled the picture themselves.

A safety manager opening the day needed to know what was overdue, who was at risk, and whether the crew on site was actually qualified. That information lived in incidents, observations, inspections, hazards, directories, spreadsheets, and physical cards. The product made them navigate. It did not help them decide.

Current

  1. Arrive on site / open laptop
  2. Open Incidents tool
  3. Leave → Observations
  4. Leave → Inspections
  5. Call office about a cert
  6. Search spreadsheet / Directory
  7. Assemble a mental picture
  8. Then act

Desired

  1. Open Safety Hub
  2. See what needs attention today
  3. Drill into the record
  4. Act
  5. On site: tap helmet / scan Safety Card
  6. Verify credentials
03 · Why this problem matters

User. Business. Product.

User

Time spent assembling, not deciding

Safety managers and superintendents spent the start of the day hunting across tools. Supervisors delayed work while they confirmed OSHA, first aid, or equipment certifications by phone.

Business

Safety data was in Procore. The program was not.

Procore later said safety data lived across disconnected systems and spreadsheets, making it nearly impossible to identify patterns or act quickly on emerging risks. That is an adoption and risk problem, not a missing chart.

Product

IA around tools, not decisions

The existing experience was organized by module: Incidents, Observations, Inspections, Directory. Users think in “what is open, who is qualified, what do I do next.”

“The problem wasn’t that users couldn’t find information. The problem was that Procore made them assemble the information themselves.”

Working insight, used as the brief for both tracks
04 · Research without the research theater

What we needed to learn, then evidence.

Safety Hub Phase 2 was a formal study. Qualifications was customer-request-driven. Both answered the same four questions.

01

What information do they actually need?

Open hazards, overdue inspections, incident accountability by contractor, expiration status, proof attachments, tag type rules.

02

At what point in the workflow?

Morning triage on web or mobile. Gate check on site. Admin bulk-assign before a shift. Not after they have already left the record.

03

What causes them to leave?

No drill-through from a card. Labels that look like project counts. A two-bar chart that does not equal “hours lost.” A missing expiration date on a tag that requires one.

04

What decision are they trying to make?

“Which site is driving incidents?” “Can this person work today?” “What is still open and who owns it?”

Safety Hub evidence. Unmoderated UserTesting study: 12 construction/safety professionals, static Figma prototype, ~20–25 min, screened for weekly dashboard use. 11/12 wanted to drill into open hazards; 0/12 could. 8/12 misread “Project Total: 247.” 11/12 understood Incidents per Manpower Log on first pass. 4/12 correctly read Time Lost’s two-bar model.

Qualifications evidence. Customer requests and field feedback, not a formal UXR plan. The recurring job: verify on site; operate hundreds to 1,000+ workers from the office; configure tag behavior once so every downstream form is consistent.

“Next steps I would do is drill into open items, what’s high risk, who owns it, and why it’s still open.”

05 · Current-state journey

The opportunity was eliminating movement.

The biggest opportunity was not prettier cards. It was removing the trips between tools before a decision could happen.

Safety Hub trigger. Morning: user needs a portfolio or project safety picture. Opens project → Incidents → leaves → Observations → leaves → Inspections → maybe Hazards → maybe a spreadsheet. Context dies at every hop. System state is unclear: is 247 projects or assessments?

Qualifications trigger. Gate or task assignment: is this person qualified today? Call the office, or hunt Directory and attachments. Meanwhile office admins manage tags that behave differently with no single grammar.

06 · The strategic insight

From information retrieval to decision support.

Design principle for both products

Stop asking “where is it.” Start answering “what do I need to know to act.”

Old mental modelWhere is the incident? Where is the cert? Which tool has inspections?

New mental modelWhat needs attention today? Can this person work? What happens if I tap through?

Safety Hub became a command center, then cards that drill into source tools without starting over. Qualifications became one grammar: configure tags, operate the workers table, verify on the Safety Card. Same principle, two surfaces of the safety program.

07 · Explore multiple solutions

Three directions. One winner per job.

Safety Hub. The job was portfolio and project triage.

Concept A

Centralized mega-dashboard

Everything visible at once. High visibility. Adds a new destination and more scanning before action.

Concept B

Contextual side panel only

Stay in the current tool. Preserves context. Too little space for cross-module risk.

Chosen · Concept C

Hub + drill-through cards

Dedicated safety home, then cards that open the record in place. Lowest context-switching without inventing a second product.

Qualifications. The job was verify in the field and operate at table scale.

Concept A

Directory-only tags

Cheap to ship. Fails on site. No NFC, no bulk, no tag grammar.

Concept B

Mobile card only

Great for a gate. Collapses when 1,000 workers need dates and attachments.

Chosen · Concept C

One grammar, three surfaces

Tags Management configures. Workers Table operates. Safety Card verifies. Rules flow downhill.

08 · The messy middle

The final design came from logged deviations, not magic.

Safety Hub v1 · Command center

One 3-tap pattern across Incidents, Observations, Inspections, Hazards: hub → category → project list → record. Different data shapes, same depth. Problem this solved: module-hopping. Risk: over-flattening modules that are not actually the same.

Safety Hub v2 · Five cards, including a known wrong chart

Spec asked for a semicircle on Daily Hazard Assessments. First draft shipped to research as a 360° donut — logged, not hidden. Research killed it. Revert plus label, YTD, and legend fixes. Time Lost’s two-bar model failed (4/12). Incidents stayed structurally unchanged (11/12).

Qualifications · Tag identity first

Editing a qualification locks the tag. Expiration and attachments stay editable. Changing the tag silently would be identity drift on an audit trail. Delete copy matches blast radius: tag vs category.

09 · Enterprise complexity

Permissions, states, scale, integrity.

Permissions

Safety Hub: company vs project views for directors vs superintendents. Qualifications: Admin to write NFC; Standard/Admin to assign tags. Public docs: Directory Admin to write chips.

States

Empty (“There Are No Assessments to Display Right Now”). Error + Try Again. Expired / expiring soon / no-expiry. Tag assigned with no supporting data. Partial loads on cards.

Scale

Workers Table designed for 500–1,000+ rows: bulk assign, CSV export, shared date vs per-worker dates. Cards must remain scannable at portfolio, not only a single project.

Data integrity

Tag is identity; lock on edit. Type-to-confirm when deleting a tag that removes it from every profile. Distinct errors: missing tag vs missing required date.

10 · The design system story

Reuse first. Extend only with a reason.

I did not treat Procore’s system as a sticker sheet. Cards, tables, toasts, destructive confirms, and empty states had to be the same grammar as the rest of the product so engineering could ship without a one-off kit.

Existing patterns: dashboard cards, side panels, data tables, mobile list → detail. Gaps: a guess-free statistical card (legend + timeframe + drill), a qualification row that encodes expiry rules, NFC write confirmation.

12 · Interaction details

Why this control, not a prettier one.

Filtering · Workers Table

Filter by expiration and tag because the job is “who is non-compliant,” not “who exists in Directory.” A generic search box would hide the compliance question.

Bulk actions · shared date vs per-worker

Bulk assign exists because 1,000 rows cannot be clicked one-by-one. Shared date is the default for a class/course; per-worker dates exist because reality is messy. Forcing one model would create silent bad data.

Information hierarchy · cards

Primary number, timeframe, then legend with count and percent. Users misread unlabeled totals. Hierarchy is a comprehension fix, not decoration.

Inline vs modal · tag edit

Lock the tag in place. Opening a modal to “change type” would invite identity drift. Expiration and attachments stay on the record.

Confirmation · matched to blast radius

Delete tag: type-to-confirm, removes from every profile. Delete category: destructive, lower severity. NFC write: progress to 100% before success. Reversible where the data model allows; irreversible where it does not, and said plainly.

13 · Validation

Prototype → test → observation → iteration.

Decisions were not taste. The donut went to research on purpose. Cards that worked were left alone.

Users understood Incidents per Manpower Log (11/12) and named the use case unprompted: which contractor is driving incidents. No structural change. Users did not understand Time Lost’s two-bar model (4/12). Redesigned to a single-series hours-lost bar. Claimed to Date confused scale ($924M) and frame (billing vs injury). Retitled, dropped prototype value, added trend. Toolbox Talk: Total vs Attended collided. Single-series attendance rate.

Avoided “users loved it.” The study produced failure modes, then briefs.

14 · Engineering collaboration

Safety Hub cards drill into source tools rather than duplicating record UIs. That is an architecture choice: reuse Incidents/Observations/Inspections instead of a parallel object model. Qualifications tag rules are configured once so mobile and web do not each hard-code exceptions.

16 · What I got wrong

I optimized for density and clever charts.

I assumed experienced Procore users would decode a donut and a two-bar “time lost” model because they live in dashboards. Testing showed extra encoding increased decision time and invented meaning (project count, contractor billing, “total” vs “attended”).

Lesson. Enterprise users do not need more information on the card. They need the right number, a timeframe, a path into the record, and a chart that does not ask them to do math. Shipping the donut to research was the recovery. Pretending I had always wanted a semicircle would have been the real mistake.

17 · Final reflection

Own the product area, not a screen.

Product

Fragmentation, not discoverability

The core problem was workflow fragmentation across safety tools and credential tracking, not a missing search field.

Design

Context beat another destination

A hub with drill-through, and a card that writes NFC, reduced cognitive load more than dumping every metric on one canvas.

A simpler workflow so construction professionals spend less time navigating Procore, and more time making safety and compliance decisions.
